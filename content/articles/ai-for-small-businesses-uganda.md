Picture a tailoring shop in Mbarara in the second week of January. School uniform orders are piling up, the owner's phone buzzes every few minutes with the same three questions (how much, how long, do you deliver), and the order book is a mix of a paper notebook and mobile money messages. Nobody in that shop needs a lecture on artificial intelligence. What they need is an extra pair of hands for the typing.

That is the honest place to start. Today's AI chat tools are useful for small businesses mainly as writing and organising assistants. They are not oracles, accountants or lawyers. This guide works through the everyday tasks where they help, a worked example, and the costs and risks you should plan for before you start.

## First, know who your customers are

Before choosing any tool, look at how your customers actually reach you. According to the [Uganda Communications Commission's market report for April to June 2026](https://www.ucc.co.ug/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/UCC-Market-Report-for-Q2-2026-Jun-2026.pdf), Uganda had 20.5 million smartphones on its networks, alongside 24.8 million feature phones and 13.6 million basic phones. The same report counts 19.7 million active mobile internet subscriptions and 37.8 million active mobile money subscriptions, with 2.55 billion mobile money transactions in the quarter.

The practical lesson: many of your customers will reach you by call, SMS or mobile money rather than by app. AI can help you prepare what you say to them, but your business should never depend on every customer having a smartphone and data.

## What AI tools are good at, and what they are not

Free AI chat assistants are good at drafting, rewording, summarising, translating rough text and turning messy notes into tidy lists. They are weak at facts they cannot check. The US National Institute of Standards and Technology describes the problem as "confabulation": the production of confidently stated but false content, often called hallucinations ([NIST AI 600-1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)). Google's own guidance for its Gemini tools tells users to review an output "to ensure clarity, relevance, and accuracy" before putting it into action ([Google Workspace](https://workspace.google.com/resources/ai/writing-effective-prompts/)).

So treat the AI as a fast junior assistant whose work you always read before it goes out.

## Task 1: Answering the same customer questions

Most small businesses answer the same handful of questions dozens of times a week. You do not need AI to fix this; you need saved replies. The free WhatsApp Business app lets you store up to 50 quick replies and pull them up by typing "/" in a chat ([WhatsApp Help Centre](https://faq.whatsapp.com/1791149784551042/?cms_platform=android)).

Where AI helps is in writing those replies well. Ask an assistant something like: "I run a tailoring shop. Write five short, friendly WhatsApp replies for: price list for school uniforms, how long orders take, delivery within town, payment by mobile money, and what to do if the fit is wrong. Keep each under 40 words." Then edit the prices, times and policies yourself, because the AI does not know them.

Meta has started offering an AI agent that replies to WhatsApp customers for businesses, but its help page says it "is currently only available in limited countries to select businesses" ([WhatsApp Help Centre](https://faq.whatsapp.com/1153795669452207/?cms_platform=web)). Do not plan around automated replies you cannot yet get, and be cautious about letting any AI answer customers unsupervised.

## Task 2: Writing posters, descriptions and price lists

AI is quick at producing a first draft of a product description, a social media post or a notice for your stall. Give it the facts (what you sell, who it is for, your location, opening hours) and ask for two or three versions in different tones. Pick one and rewrite it in your own voice.

Many Ugandan customers prefer Luganda or another local language. Google added Luganda to Google Translate in 2022, but Google itself said the new languages used a technique that "isn't perfect" ([Google](https://blog.google/products/translate/24-new-languages/)). Use translation tools for a rough draft only, and have a fluent speaker check anything a customer will read. A badly translated price or promise costs more than it saves.

## Task 3: Making sense of your records

If your sales live in a notebook and a stream of mobile money messages, AI can help you design a simple record-keeping sheet. Ask: "Design a simple sales record for a small shop in a spreadsheet: columns for date, item, quantity, price, payment method and customer type. Suggest three weekly totals I should check." Then build it yourself in Google Sheets or Excel.

Once you have a few weeks of figures, you can ask the AI to suggest patterns to look for, such as which days are busiest. Two rules apply here:

- **Do the sums yourself or with spreadsheet formulas.** Chat tools can make arithmetic mistakes and state them confidently.
- **Strip out personal details first.** Remove customer names and phone numbers before pasting anything into an AI tool (more on why below).

## Task 4: Thinking through a decision

An AI assistant can be a useful sparring partner when you are planning: "What questions should I ask before renting a second sewing machine?" or "List the costs I might be forgetting when I start delivering outside town." It will give you a checklist to think with.

It should not be the source of facts about tax, licensing or the law. Rules change, and AI tools may be out of date or simply wrong. Confirm anything official with the relevant authority or a qualified professional. The same applies to money: AI tools cannot tell you whether an investment is safe, and nobody can promise returns. Our piece on [risk before reward](/insights/risk-before-reward) explains why that caution matters.

## A worked example: one week at the tailoring shop

Here is how the hypothetical Mbarara tailor could use a free AI assistant over one busy week, spending perhaps an hour in total.

1. **Monday:** Write down, by hand, the ten questions customers asked most last week. Ask the AI to draft short replies to each. Correct the prices and times, then save them as WhatsApp Business quick replies.
2. **Tuesday:** Ask the AI to draft a notice about the uniform order deadline in English. Ask a Luganda-speaking friend to translate or check a translated version before posting it as a WhatsApp status.
3. **Wednesday:** Ask the AI to design a one-page order form with columns for measurements, fabric, deposit paid and collection date. Print it or copy it into a notebook.
4. **Thursday:** Copy last month's sales into a spreadsheet with names removed. Ask the AI which simple totals would show whether uniforms or alterations earn more, then calculate them with spreadsheet formulas.
5. **Friday:** Ask the AI for a checklist of things to agree with a school before taking a bulk order (deposit, delivery date, size list, what happens with returns). Use it as a prompt for your own conversation, not as a contract.

Notice what the AI never does in this example: talk to customers on its own, handle personal data, set prices or make the final decision.

## The costs and risks to plan for

### Data and airtime

AI chat tools need a working internet connection, and long sessions with images or voice use more data than text. The ITU notes that mobile broadband users in low-income economies spend about 22 times more of their income on it than users in high-income economies ([ITU](https://www.itu.int/itu-d/reports/statistics/2025/10/15/ff25-affordability-of-ict-services)). Keep prompts short, stick to text, and do your bigger AI sessions where Wi-Fi is cheaper. If you are curious how shared connections work, see our explainer on [how community Wi-Fi works](/insights/how-community-wifi-works).

### Privacy and Uganda's data protection law

Customer names, phone numbers, measurements and payment details are personal data. Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act requires informed consent before personal data is collected or processed, requires organisations to keep it secure, limits transfers of personal data outside Uganda, and requires data collectors, processors and controllers to register with the Personal Data Protection Office ([DLA Piper summary](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=UG)). The same summary notes fines and, for companies, penalties of up to 2% of annual gross turnover.

Most popular AI tools are run by companies outside Uganda. Google, for example, says a subset of Gemini chats are read by human reviewers and advises users not to enter anything "that's confidential or that you wouldn't want a reviewer to see" ([Google Gemini Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961)). The simple rule: never paste customer personal data into a general AI chat tool. If your business handles a lot of personal data, take proper legal advice on registration and compliance. This article is general information, not legal advice.

### Accuracy

Read every output. Check every number. Never forward an AI-written message to a customer without reading it first.

### Scams

The same tools are used by fraudsters to write convincing fake messages and payment requests. Train yourself and any staff to spot them with our guide on how to [recognise AI-generated scams](/insights/recognise-ai-generated-scams).

## Where to start this week

- Pick one repetitive writing task, such as customer replies, and use AI only for that.
- Set a rule that no personal data goes into AI tools.
- Read and edit everything before it reaches a customer.
- After two weeks, ask yourself honestly whether it saved time. If not, stop.

Whether you pay for mobile data or for a Wi-Fi data voucher, every megabyte counts. Much of the data a phone uses goes on things you did not ask for: videos that play by themselves, photos that download automatically and apps that update in the background. The settings below help you take control.

Menu names differ slightly between phone makers and app versions. If you cannot find a setting exactly as described, search for it using the search bar at the top of your phone's Settings app.

## 1. See where your data goes

Start by finding out which apps use the most data. On most Android phones, open **Settings**, then **Network and internet** or **Connections**, then **Data usage**. You will see a list of apps and how much each has used. Focus your effort on the top few.

## 2. Turn on Data Saver

Android has a built-in **Data Saver** that stops most apps from using data in the background when you are on mobile data. You can usually find it under **Settings**, **Network and internet**, **Data Saver**. You can still allow specific apps, such as your messaging app, to work normally.

## 3. Stop WhatsApp downloading everything

In WhatsApp, open **Settings**, then **Storage and data**. Under **Media auto-download**, choose what downloads automatically on mobile data and on Wi-Fi. Many people set photos only, or nothing at all, on mobile data, and tap to download the rest when they need it. In the same menu, the **Use less data for calls** option reduces data used by voice and video calls.

## 4. Lower video quality

Video is usually the biggest data user. In the YouTube app, open **Settings**, then **Video quality preferences**, and choose **Data saver** for mobile networks. You can always raise the quality for a single video when you need to.

Turn off autoplay in video and social media apps too, so the next video does not start playing by itself.

## 5. Update apps over Wi-Fi only

App updates can be large. In the Google Play Store, tap your profile picture, then **Settings**, **Network preferences**, **Auto-update apps**, and choose **Over Wi-Fi only**.

## 6. Download once, use many times

If you have access to a Wi-Fi connection, use it to download things you will need again: offline maps, music, podcasts and documents. Many apps offer an offline or download option for exactly this reason.

## 7. Close what you do not need

Apps you no longer use can still sync in the background. Uninstall them, or restrict their background data from the app's page in **Settings**.

## A final tip

After changing these settings, check the data usage screen again after a few days. You will see which changes made the biggest difference, and whether any app still needs attention.

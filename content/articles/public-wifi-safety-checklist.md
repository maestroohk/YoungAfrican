Public and community Wi-Fi can make getting online much cheaper. Like any shared space, it works best when everyone follows a few simple habits. None of the steps below need technical skill, and most take only a few seconds.

## Check the network name

Criminals sometimes set up a network with a name that looks like a real one, hoping people will join it by mistake. Before you connect, confirm the exact network name with the venue or the voucher seller. Be wary of two networks with nearly identical names in the same place.

## Know what the login page should ask for

A genuine hotspot login page asks for a voucher code, and sometimes for an access option such as a time pass or data bundle. It should never ask for:

- Your mobile money PIN.
- Your email or social media password.
- Your bank card details, unless the operator has clearly told you it offers online payment.

If a page asks for any of these unexpectedly, close it and disconnect.

## Look for the padlock

When you visit a website, check that its address starts with https and that your browser shows a padlock. That means the connection between your phone and the website is encrypted, so other people on the same Wi-Fi cannot read what you send. Most major websites and apps already use encryption, but it is worth checking before you type a password.

## Protect your codes and PINs

Never share one-time codes sent by SMS, mobile money PINs or passwords with anyone, including people who say they work for a network or a bank. Genuine support staff do not need them. Mobile money transactions that use USSD codes go over the mobile network rather than Wi-Fi, but the same rule applies: your PIN is yours alone.

## Change a few phone settings

- **Turn off automatic joining for open networks** you do not trust, so your phone does not connect to them by itself.
- **Forget networks you no longer use.** Open the Wi-Fi list, tap the network and choose **Forget**.
- **Keep your phone and apps updated.** Updates fix security problems that criminals try to exploit.
- **Use a screen lock** so nobody can use your phone if it is lost or stolen.

## Be careful with sharing

Some phones can share files with nearby devices. Turn file sharing and Bluetooth off when you are not using them, especially in busy places.

## When something feels wrong

If a network behaves strangely, for example by showing unexpected pop-ups, redirecting you to unfamiliar sites or asking for personal details, disconnect straight away. Tell the venue or the hotspot operator so they can check the equipment. If you think you have shared a PIN or password, change it immediately and contact your mobile money provider or bank.
